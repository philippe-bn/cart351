1. ***Face De Book* by Kerven-Laurent Casimir** <br>
A fake social media page on which the user can type in fields and have the information returned to the screen in a phishing scam manner. It issues a critique of social media's data collection (specifically pointing to Facebook), and the risks related to giving your private information to these databases and what can happen if one is not careful. <br>
I loved the design of the page, it looked very clean and professional which helped sell the concept. I found that the handling of the data could have been a bit more interesting, to further push the concept of a phishing scam and better relate to the readings. <br>
It would be nice to compile users' information into a JSON file and make it so users can "pay" on a button and see all the other users' "private" information. I also agree with Jack's idea of adding an incentive to log in. <br>

2. ***myspace* by Nerly Cadet** <br>
An imitation of the social media *myspace*, for users to write and select information about themselves, hyperstylicized in pink and a variety of textures. The information is then returned as a "profile" and saved in a JSON file. <br>
I loved the hyperstylicized aesthetic which took the attention away from how much personal data the user is entering. I liked that it compiled users' information into a database. The clear labelling of the fields made it very intuitive. I didn't love how simplistic the form response displayed was. <br>
It would be interesting to see other users' profiles on a homepage, and be able to give "likes" to other users' fields. Maybe users could log in? <br>

3. ***Cloud Reading* by Ben Macknofsky** <br>
A daily cloud reading website where all users are invited to speculate on cloud shapes. The data is collected and then displayed by popularity on the site. <br>
I adore the concept and the execution. Everything was extremely clear and related directly to the theoretical framework. I especially loved the early web aesthetic and how the piece felt like a simpler time and gave me this sense of nostalgia while incorporating these ideas that are more recent (Wordle, Krillion). I agree with Sabine's comment that having the interpretations previously suggested as displayed right away limits the imaginative capacities of the user... <br>
I think the idea that Lanna mentioned where the goal is to find the rarest interpretation of the cloud like in Krillion is a good idea, and would definitely be fun! <br>
